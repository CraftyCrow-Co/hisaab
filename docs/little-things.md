# Little things you can already do

*A companion to the Guide. Nothing here needs switching on. It all comes from how the
sheet is built. Skim it once, and you'll keep finding uses.*

---

## Names that do the work

**A slash makes a group.** `bank/idfc`, `bank/kotak`. Now `bank` totals both, and
`bank/idfc` totals one. You never set up a list of categories. Your naming did it.

**Go as deep as you like.** `mom/union/fd` counts three ways: under `mom`, under
`mom/union`, and on its own. No setup, no limit.

**Departments, teams and branches, for free.** `staff/salary`, `staff/wages`,
`staff/bonus`. The word `staff` now totals everyone you pay. You never set up a
department. You just named things the same way.

**One sheet, many lives.** `personal/…`, `shop/…`, `mom/…`. The first word keeps
them apart. Look at one, or at everything.

**The dropdown remembers every name you've used.** Start typing and pick from the
list. That's the whole trick to clean totals: never retype a name from memory.

**A name can be anything.** A person, a bank, a shop, a machine, a project code
like `#PROJ-123`. The sheet doesn't care what it is. It just counts it.

**Changed your mind about a name? Ctrl+H.** Find `Acme`, replace with
`Acme Pvt Ltd`, and every row changes at once. Split totals join up, old names
get updated, and nothing about what happened changes. *(Select the columns first
and tick "specific range", so it only touches what you picked. Do it in both Log
and Recurring, or tomorrow's rows come back with the old name.)*

**So you can start rough.** Renaming takes two minutes, not a project. Start with
rough names today, tidy them in December, and your whole history updates.

**Fix a spelling and the totals join up.** `Swiggy` and `swiggy ` (with a stray
space) were two names splitting one number. One replace, and it's one number
again.

## Words that count

**A tag totals every row that carries it.** Put `sharma-reception` on the advance,
the ingredients and the staff, and the sheet adds them up. The sign decides plus
or minus.

**Several words on one row.** `tiffins, packaging`: the full amount counts in
both. Two totals from one row, no extra work.

**Anything can be a tag.** A client, a season (`diwali-2026`), a channel
(`instagram`), an experiment, one big order. If you'll want to judge it later, tag
it.

**A job that runs across months still adds up.** Advance in May, costs in June,
final payment in July. The tag's total is the real number. Set the period to FY or
All so a single month doesn't mislead you.

**Cash Flow shows who pays you and where it goes,** side by side, with each
one's share. You'll see where the money really goes.

## The sign, and the two ends

**Minus means it left you. Plus means it came to you.** That's all. The colour
matches: green in, red out.

**You never tell the sheet which names are "you".** Ravi gives you ₹5,000:
`ravi → cash, +5,000`. You pay it back: `cash → ravi, −5,000`. Same names,
opposite signs.

**Moving your own money isn't income.** Bank to cash is one row: the amount as a
minus, From the account it left, To the one it reached, Purpose **Self**. No
report counts it as earnings.

**Starting balances have their own mode.** Mode **Opening Balance** keeps
day-one money out of your income reports. The balance still counts it.

**Mode gives you a second view of everything:** cash, bank, card or UPI, without
tagging anything.

**Description is free text.** Write anything, or nothing. It never changes a
total.

## Dates let you see ahead

**Type a future date and read the balance beside it.** That's your money on that
day if things go as planned. That's most of what "forecasting" means.

**Add a planned row to try out a decision.** A ₹2 L machine in October, a client's
₹1 L in August: the months ahead update straight away. Delete the row and
everything goes back.

**Planned rows never change your balance.** Money isn't real until it arrives,
so expected money is shown but not counted.

**Two dates are kept.** *Planned on* is when it was due. *Date* is when it
happened. The gap is how the sheet learns who pays late. It shows as
**Avg Waiting Time** on the Chase List.

**The same tick handles linked rows.** When two rows are linked, exactly one side
is ticked. A purchase paid in instalments: the *plan* is ticked, the payments are
real. One bill covering two jobs: the *bill* is real, the *parts* are ticked.
Whatever isn't a payment on its own gets the tick, so the money is counted once.
*Guide, sections 6 and 14.*

**Enter next year's rows today.** School fees you know are coming, a wedding in
March, an insurance renewal. Put them in once and stop carrying them in your head.

**Backdate freely.** Forgot a month? Type the rows with their real dates. The
balance and every report sort themselves out.

**A date can include a time.** `06-Aug 11:30`, and the balance beside it is your
money at 11:30, not at the end of the day. For a shop, that's the morning float,
the afternoon rush and the evening deposit, each in the right order.

**Rows on the same day stay in order.** Ten entries on one date sort in the order
they happened, so the running balance is right on every line, not just at the end
of the day.

**Ctrl+Shift+Alt+; enters the date and time right now,** in one keystroke.
(Ctrl+; is just the date. Ctrl+Shift+; is just the time.)

## Recurring — write once, runs by itself

**One row covers a year.** Rent, salaries, EMIs, subscriptions, a monthly
retainer. Never type them twelve times.

**Unusual patterns are covered.** Every 2 weeks, the third Friday, the last day,
both Day 1 *and* Day 15, every 4 months. Pick from the dropdowns.

**End is flexible.** Blank means never. A number means that many times. A date
means stop then.

**Change one month without breaking the rule.** Date moved, paid in cash instead?
Change that one row. The rule never overwrites your edit.

**Every rule shows its monthly bite.** ₹18,000 a year of insurance is ₹1,500 a
month. Now you can compare anything with anything.

**EMIs count down by themselves.** Paid, remaining, next date, and a bar filling
up. No separate loan tracker.

**Mark each rule Essential, Nice-to-have or Can drop.** In a tight month you
already know what to cut first, and how much a month it saves.

**One number worth a look:** **Safety money** on the Dashboard. With months to
survive set to 12, it's a year of what goes out whether you work or not. Most
people cancel something after seeing it.

## Watchlist — the fastest thing in the sheet

**Type any name and see its whole story.** In, out, net, money life, and a trend
line. A client, an account, a tag, a project code.

**Add a row to compare.** Two clients, two months, two lines of work, side by
side, with no report to build.

**Ready-made periods.** This month or this year, the next or last 30 days, 90 days,
6 months or a year, and more. Or leave Period blank and set your own From and To
dates.

**Ask a question, get the answer, delete the row.** Use it like scrap paper.
Nothing you type there can break anything.

**Group names work here.** Type `staff` and everyone you pay is in one number.

## Limits and goals

**A limit is a ceiling. A goal is a floor.** "Chai under ₹500 a week." "₹30 L from
events this year." Both go in the same small table.

**Point them at anything:** a name, a tag, an account. Not just spending
categories.

**Rolling windows, not just calendar months.** "Last 30 days" moves forward with
you.

**Colour is the alert.** The bar fills green, and anything over the limit shows
red. No pop-ups, no nagging.

**Break the same limit three times and it suggests a better one,** worked out from
your own spending: *"₹900 is more realistic."* A budget you always break is the
wrong number, not a lack of discipline.

## Reading the numbers

**The Chase List is the most valuable list in the sheet.** It's money you've
already earned but not collected. Collecting it is easier than finding a new
customer.

**It learns how your clients pay.** Once there's history, it shows how late each
one usually pays, so you can plan around it.

**The monthly snapshot colours itself.** A month that won't cover what it needs
turns amber, then red, months before it arrives, while you can still act.

**Last year is in the same table.** This September next to last September, with no
extra work.

**Where the profit went, in three lines.** Invested, Loan, Took home: the three
things that take cash without being a cost. This answers "we made a profit, so
where's the money?"

**The report checks itself.** Income that isn't tagged to any line of work shows up
as its own figure, with the rows listed under Missing Counts. Tag them and it
clears.

**Untick Is Planned and the row leaves the Chase List.** The due date is kept, so
the sheet remembers how late it was.

**Paid in parts?** Add what came, and reduce the planned row to what's still due.
The Chase List keeps tracking the rest. Or link each part to the planned row and
the sheet keeps count for you: *paid ₹2.5 L, ₹1.5 L left*. *Guide, section 14.*

## Free, because it's a spreadsheet

All of these are Google Sheets features, not Hisaab's. You already have them.

### Entering and fixing

**Ctrl+D copies the row above.** Most rows are nearly the same as the last one.

**It finishes names for you.** Start typing in a column and it suggests what
you've typed before. Enter accepts, Escape ignores it. That stops two spellings of
one client.

**Ctrl+Shift+V pastes plain text.** Copying from WhatsApp or a bank statement
brings their colours and fonts along. This keeps only the text.

**Drag the small blue square** at a cell's corner to fill dates or repeat a name
down twenty rows.

**Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes.** And version history is behind both.

### Getting around

**Ctrl+F finds anything.** A name, an amount, part of a description. Faster than
any filter.

**Ctrl+↓ jumps to the last row.** The bottom of a 2,000-row Log, instantly.

**Click a column heading to sort.** Sort the Log by amount and your biggest
payments are at the top.

**Filter views let you slice without changing the view for others.** This month,
one account, untagged rows: saved, reusable, and only you see them.

**Select some amounts and look at the bottom-right corner.** It shows the sum.
Ctrl+click to add scattered rows. Click it to switch to average, count or
highest.

**Hide the columns you never use.** Ref Num, link: right-click, Hide. Nothing is
deleted. The sheet just looks simpler.

**Group rows to fold them away.** Old months collapse behind a small +. The history
stays; it's just out of the way.

**Open the same file in two windows.** Log on one screen, Dashboard on the other,
both live. Type a row and watch the numbers change.

### Working with people

**Right-click a row → Comment.** Add the invoice number, a photo link, or "ask
Ramesh about this" to the row itself.

**Put @their-email in a comment** and it's assigned to them. They get an email,
they reply on the row, and it's marked done when finished. *"@ramesh what was this
₹40,000?"* Your questions stop getting lost in WhatsApp.

**Share it with your CA as view-only.** They see everything and can't change
anything. No exports, no emails, no confusion about versions.

**Get an email when anyone edits.** Tools → Notification settings. Useful once
staff start entering rows.

### Keeping it safe and taking it with you

**It's on your phone.** Same file, in the Google Sheets app. Add a row standing in
the shop.

**It works offline.** Turn on offline mode in the app, and type at the godown or on
a flight. It syncs when you're back online.

**Download as PDF:** the Dashboard on one page, for the bank or your CA.

**Download as Excel or CSV** for a backup that doesn't depend on Google.

**Make a copy any time.** It's a snapshot of your finances on that date, kept
forever. A free year-end archive. A copy's automation doesn't run until you use
**Actions → Set up this copy**, and an archive doesn't need it.

**Undo goes back as far as you like.** File → Version history → go back to any
day. You can't permanently break this file, so try things.

### Making it yours

**Add a tab for anything.** Right-click at the bottom, add a sheet, name it. Which
nominee is on which account, policy numbers, where the locker key is, who to call
when something goes wrong. Most families keep this on one piece of paper and lose
it. A business has ten times more of it and nowhere to keep it. Now it sits next to
the money it's about, in the file you already open. *Names, numbers and nominees,
yes. Passwords and OTPs, never, here or anywhere.*

**Add your own formulas.** Add a column, write a SUM, build the one view only you
need. It's your file.

**Add your own colour rules.** Conditional formatting: "anything over ₹50,000
turns yellow." The sheet starts highlighting what you care about.

**Set your locale to India, once.** File → Settings. Then 06/08 reads as 6 August
and ₹ works properly. Two clicks that prevent a year of confusion.

## Safety net

**One check catches every kind of mistake.** On Wealth, under Checks, type what you
actually have. The gap is whatever's missing: a bounced EMI, a forgotten sale, a
typo.

**A bounced payment is quick to fix.** Tick Is Planned again and move the date
forward. It goes back to "coming", and the balance corrects itself.

**Roughly right is better than missing.** One row, *−40,000 · bank → misc ·
"catching up June"*, and you're up to date. No streaks to keep.

**Tags and words are yours to change any time.** Keep what happened true: date,
amount, who paid whom. Everything else can be changed whenever you like.
