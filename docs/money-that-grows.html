Money that grows

Part three. The Guide answered the quick question: "do I have enough?" This one answers the slow one: is it growing?

Investments, loans and building wealth need no new skill and no new way of writing a row. It all works with what you already know: a sign, two names and a Purpose. There's one tab you haven't used yet, Wealth, and every number on it comes from rows you were already writing.

Spent or parked

Two rows can both be a minus and mean opposite things.

₹450 on dinner is gone. ₹1,00,000 into an FD is still yours. It's just somewhere else. Your bank balance fell the same way both times. Your wealth fell only once.

That difference is what this page is about, and one dropdown records it:

  • Running cost or Specific cost: spent. It made you poorer.
  • Invested: parked. It moved somewhere, but it's still yours.

Pick it when you enter the row and you never have to think about it again. The reports keep the two apart from then on.

Not every minus is a cost.

Every place gets a name

Anywhere your money sits and waits is just a name in To, like a shop, a client or a bank.

fd/hdfc        mf/parag        gold           epf
fd/sbi         mf/quant        plot/nashik    nps
                stocks/zerodha

The slash works as it always does: mf totals every fund, fd totals every deposit, and each one still counts on its own. You never build a portfolio list. Your naming builds it.

Business things that last work the same way: machine/oven, deposit/shop, laptop.

Buying and selling

Buying is one row. Money leaves the bank and goes to the holding.

DateAmountFromToPurpose
05-Aug−1,00,000bank/hdfcfd/hdfcInvested

Paying in parts? A ₹5 L machine, paid in three payments whenever you had the cash. Enter the whole purchase once as a planned row, then link each payment to it as it happens: paid ₹3 L, ₹2 L left. Only the plan is ticked, so the money is counted once. Guide, section 14.

Selling, or an FD maturing, takes two rows. The second row is the important one.

DateAmountFromToPurposeTags
05-Aug+1,00,000fd/hdfcbank/hdfcInvested
05-Aug+7,000fd/hdfcbank/hdfcEarnedreturns

The first is your own money coming back. The second is what it earned. Getting your own money back isn't income. Only the ₹7,000 is. Split them once, when you enter them, and every report stays right.

Money coming back and money earned are never the same row.

The one number that tells you where you stand

Open Watchlist, type a holding's name, and set the period to All.

fd/hdfc      out 1,00,000    in 1,07,000    net    +7,000
mf/parag     out 3,60,000    in         0   net −3,60,000

Read it like this:

  • Net is negative: it's still running. That figure is what you have put in.
  • Net is positive: it's finished. That figure is what it earned you.

The sign tells you which.

While it's running, Net is what you have in it. When it's finished, Net is what it made.

One row per holding, and you're looking at your portfolio. No report to build.

Debt is the same thing, backwards

Borrowing is a plus. Repaying is a minus. Use Purpose Loan on both, because borrowed money isn't income, however good the month looks.

Then it works the other way round:

loan/car     in 5,00,000    out 6,20,000    net −1,20,000
  • Net positive while the loan runs: that's what you still owe.
  • Net negative when it's finished: that's what borrowing cost you.

₹1,20,000, without an amortisation table.

That number is useful for one more thing. Paying a loan off early saves you the interest you would have paid. Compare that with what an investment might earn. The loan usually wins, because its saving is certain and the investment's return isn't.

All your loans are listed together on the Wealth tab, next to what you own: what's still owed, the monthly payment, and when it ends.

WHAT YOU OWE
Loan            Still owed    Per Month     Ends
loan/car          3,15,000      −18,400     Mar-2028
loan/gold         2,00,000      −12,000     Nov-2027

They sit next to your holdings on purpose. What you're worth is one minus the other, so both belong on one screen.

When the lender pays the seller directly. Most car and machine loans never pass through your bank. Write it as two rows on the same date:

DateAmountFromToPurpose
05-Aug+5,00,000loan/carbank/hdfcLoan
05-Aug−5,00,000bank/hdfcasset/carInvested

The money never really went through your bank, but writing it this way gets everything right. The two rows cancel out in your bank balance, you own a car, you owe a loan, and both show up in the right place.

Check in whenever you like

There's a small table on Wealth called Checks: the date, what you're worth that day, and a note if you like.

Date          NetWorth     Change       Notes & Strategies
12-May-2026   28,43,000
05-Aug-2026   30,53,000   +2,10,000     sold the plot
14-Nov-2026   31,10,000     +57,000

Add a row every quarter, twice a year, or whenever you remember. The sheet fills in the change since your last row.

There's no schedule to keep, and nothing goes wrong if you skip a year. Two rows already show a direction.

If your figure and the sheet's figure are different, it tells you: sheet says ₹30.5 L · you say ₹30.1 L · off by ₹40,000. The gap is almost always a holding whose value changed, or one you never entered.

Is your debt working?

Two loans can look the same on the day the money arrives and turn out completely differently. One buys you something, or helps you earn more. The other just gets spent.

The sheet never asks where the loan money went. Nobody can answer that honestly, because money gets mixed together once it's in your bank. It asks two easier questions instead.

First: are you earning more each month than your loans take each month?

You Earn         +34,000 a month more than last time
Your loans take  −30,400 a month

If yes, the debt is working, whatever it was spent on. That's the whole test. It also covers a loan taken for stock or wages, which never becomes something you own. It becomes next month's income. Judged by assets alone, that loan would look like a failure.

If not, do you at least own something new? A machine that hasn't started earning is still a machine. You bought something. You're not poorer.

Neither? The money's gone and the payments continue. That's the one to worry about.

The sheet gives its verdict in one line: "Debt that earns", "Debt that owns", or "Debt that vanished".

Good debt isn't about what you bought. It's whether you now earn more than it costs you.

One note: this looks at all your borrowing together, not loan by loan. Splitting it by loan would mean guessing which rupee went where, and a guess that looks like a fact is worse than no answer.

Record what goes in and out, not what happens inside

A broker account, a mutual fund platform, a trading app: money goes in, money comes out, and a hundred things happen inside.

Record only what crosses the edge. Money into stocks/zerodha, money out of stocks/zerodha. What happens inside is what the broker's statement is for, and they do it better. Watchlist's Net on that one name is your real result.

That's forty rows a year instead of four hundred, and more accurate, because forty is a number you'll actually keep up with.

Trading works the same way. If you're buying and selling often, you don't need a different tool. You need a narrower question. Judging the whole account? Record the edge. Judging one position? Give it its own name, like stocks/tatamotors, and record its buys and sells there. Net answers whichever question you asked.

You can do both: the edge for the account, and names for the positions you're watching closely.

SIPs are just recurring rows

One row in Recurring: −10,000 · bank → mf/parag · Monthly · Day 5 · Purpose Invested. Write it once and it keeps going, with nothing to tick.

It gives you two things:

The yearly number. ₹1,20,000 goes into investments this year, whether you notice or not. Most people have never seen that figure for themselves.

Importance: Essential. In a tight month, the sheet knows this isn't the thing to cut. Investing is mostly the habit of not stopping, so tell the sheet it isn't optional.

What counts is what moved

Money out, money back, and the difference. That's what the sheet records, and it's the only part that's certainly true, because it happened.

A gain isn't yours until it's a row.

A holding that's up 40% on screen hasn't paid you anything. That's a price, not a payment, and it could be 20% by Friday. The day you sell, two rows go in, Net turns positive, and now it's real. Selling is what turns a number into money.

So the sheet not tracking prices isn't a gap. It's the same rule as everywhere else: money isn't real until it arrives.

Except when you hold for years

There's one exception.

Something you'll hold for ten or twenty years (a fund, a plot, a policy) can go wrong quietly for years, and the Log won't show it, because no money moved. That's exactly the problem. A fund that hasn't grown since 2019 looks the same as a good one until you check outside the sheet.

So for long-term holdings only, update their value once a quarter. Not to feel good about a gain, but to answer one question: is this still worth holding, or should I change it?

That's the Wealth tab: the slow one, the only screen measured in years. Once a holding has a name, it appears here automatically, with one column for you to fill in:

HOLDINGS                                          as of  05-Aug-2026

Holdings          Invested   Last checked Value   On Paper   Returns %
mf/parag          3,60,000             4,15,000    +55,000        9.8%
gold              2,00,000             2,48,000    +48,000        3.7%
plot/nashik      18,00,000            22,00,000  +4,00,000        2.5%
stocks/zerodha    1,20,000             1,05,000    −15,000       −8.2%
epf               4,40,000                    —          —           —

Everything except one column is already known. Invested is what you've put in minus what's come back. The last two stay empty until you type a value, and fill in as soon as you do. Fill in one row, all five, or none for a year. It never nags and never breaks. Empty is fine. A holding you haven't listed shows as "missing" above the table.

The percentage is a proper yearly return. Every rupee you put into that holding is already in the Log with its date, and that's exactly what a proper return calculation needs. Your SIP into mf/parag didn't all go in on day one, and the number accounts for that. Measured roughly it reads 4.8%. Measured properly it's 9.8%. Same rows, twice the answer.

That's the column that drives the decision. ₹4,00,000 up on plot/nashik sounds good until you see it's 2.5% a year for eight years. Then it's worth a rethink.

These values stay out of the Log, on purpose. A valuation isn't money moving. Enter it as a row and your bank balance would be wrong. The same rule that keeps planned money out of your balance keeps paper gains out too.

That's also why In Hand doesn't count your investments, and shouldn't:

In Hand is what you can spend. It was never what you're worth.

What you're worth is a different total, shown at the top of the Wealth tab:

Net Worth      30,53,000      ▲ 2,10,000 since 12-May
  In hand       6,48,000      spendable today
  Parked       29,20,000      at value, where you've given one
  Owed         −5,15,000

Anything you haven't valued is counted at what you paid for it. So the number can be out of date, but it never makes you look richer than you are. Add a check each quarter and the arrow starts to mean something: not just what you're worth, but which way it's moving.

Before you invest anything

Three steps, always in this order:

  1. Cushion first. Months of cover must reach your target. The cushion isn't spare money. It's what stops a bad quarter from ending the business.
  2. Then expensive debt. You know what each loan costs you. Clear anything that costs more than a safe investment would earn, before you buy anything.
  3. Then invest, but only what's above the cushion. In Hand after your safety money is the honest answer to "can I afford this?", and it's the only money investments should come from. Past twelve months of cover, the Dashboard tells you so.

Investing the cushion is how a business fails in a good year. Nothing was wrong with the investment. The money just wasn't free.

Your investments become a line of work

Tag every return returns: interest, dividends, rent, profit on a sale.

Now look at Which work pays on Real Picture. returns shows up as a line of work of its own, with income and no work cost. It took none of your time.

tiffins      in 4,20,000 · work costs 2,52,000  →  adds 1,68,000
events       in 5,00,000 · work costs 4,35,000  →  adds    65,000
returns      in   58,000 · work costs        0  →  adds    58,000

Watch that last line over the years. It only grows if you keep adding to it.

And the finish line is defined for you, on the Wealth tab, under How financially free are you?:

Going In         ₹10,000 a month  ·  ₹1,20,000 this year
Coming Back      returns ₹58,000 this year
Costs To Exist   ₹3,36,000 this year

Your return covers 17% of your Running costs

When that reaches 100%, working becomes a choice. That's all "wealth" means here, not a number someone else picked for you. It's Months of cover, asked about your whole life instead of one bad quarter.

Seventeen percent isn't close. But it isn't zero, and it grew because you added to it. Three numbers, one page, checked four times a year.

Growing a business

Same system, different words.

Money that buys something lasting (the oven, the laptop, the shop deposit) is Purpose Invested, not a cost. That's why the Used to grow card exists, and why an owner who's had a heavy year can look poor on paper while actually building.

An experiment is a tag. bet/second-shop, marketing/instagram, product/tiffins-v2. Tag both sides: the spending, and whatever comes back. Watchlist shows the result. Net negative is what the experiment has cost so far. The month Net turns positive is when it has paid for itself. No formula needed.

Growth uses up cash before it brings cash in. The new shop's rent starts in September, and its customers arrive in January. Enter both as planned rows and the Monthly Snapshot shows you the dip, months before you're in it.

Growth is a cash problem long before it's a profit problem.

Things that look like investments but aren't

Term insurance. A cost, not an investment: Running cost. If it only pays out when something bad happens, it's a cost. That's not a criticism. It's the best money most people spend.

Endowment plans and ULIPs. Part cost, part investment, and the split is usually hidden. Mark the premium Invested and let the maturity settle it. Years from now, Watchlist's Net on that name is exactly what the whole thing earned you. Very few people ever see that number.

Your car, your phone. Parked, but losing value. Give them names if you like, but value them honestly each quarter. Don't count a five-year-old car at what you paid.

Money lent to a friend. lent/rahul, Purpose Invested. It comes back or it doesn't, and Net shows which.

The awkward ones

PF deducted by your employer. It never reached your bank, so don't record it as cash. Put the balance in the holdings value and move on. (Recording gross salary instead? Then record the deduction too, salary → epf, so the two match.)

Dividends reinvested automatically. No cash moved, so skip the row. The quarterly value picks it up.

Property. Registration, stamp duty, brokerage, and every other fee: each is another Invested row to the same name. Net becomes your true cost, the number everyone forgets.

Gold bought for a wedding. One row to gold. Twenty years of those rows is a holding nobody planned but most families have.

Six habits

  • Purpose on every row. Spent or parked. Thirty seconds a day.
  • Same name, every time. A holding split across two spellings gives two half answers.
  • Tag every return returns. One word, and your investments become a line of work.
  • Sell, then count it. A sale is two rows: your money back, and the profit. Until then it's only a price.
  • One morning a quarter to update values. Long-term holdings only, on the Wealth tab. Ten minutes, in your calendar, and add a net-worth check before you finish.
  • Never invest the cushion. Not once, not "just this time".

The Guide told you what's in the bank and what it means. Part two showed how much four columns can do. This part is the slow one: a line that barely moves for two years, and then matters more than any other.

Spend it, and it's gone. Invest it, and it's still working.

Wealth isn't what you earn. It's what you kept, and what it's doing while you sleep.
