Most finance tools would ask you for account types, entity paths, a chart of accounts, and a category tree you have to maintain forever. Hisaab asks for none of it. The hierarchy comes out of how you name things. The reports come out of one column with seven values.

That is not a spreadsheet trick. It is what is left after asking, for months, which parts of accounting are actually load-bearing and which are habit. Almost all of it turned out to be habit.

Your business has the same problem

Orders, stock, staff, cash, clients, jobs. Every one of them has a version of the question Hisaab had to answer: what is the smallest set of things I must record for everything else to fall out on its own?

The usual answer is to add screens. A field for this, a report for that, a tool per department — and two years later nobody can say where a number came from, so nobody trusts any of them.

The better answer is almost always fewer things, named correctly. Find the handful that genuinely matter, define how they relate, and let the rest be derived. That is the work — and it is the same work whether the subject is money, inventory, or how a job moves through your shop.
